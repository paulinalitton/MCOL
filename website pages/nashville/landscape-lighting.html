Landscape lighting in Nashville has to thread a specific needle. The mature hackberries and white oaks shading 12 South and Hillsboro Village deserve trunk uplights, but the lot lines on those streets run tight — push the beam an inch too wide and you are spilling into a neighbor's bedroom window. We design every low-voltage LED system on site at dusk, walking the property with the homeowner so the layered nightscape lands inside the property line and stays there.

The clay-heavy soil that holds water all winter east of I-65 also shapes the install. We route home-runs above the frost-affected layer where we can, trench narrow and tight against bed edges in Sylvan Park and Belmont, and pull conduit through crawlspaces when a Germantown row house leaves no other option. Fixtures are solid brass or copper, plant-safe, fully hidden by day — and aimed so the only thing that glows after sunset is the canopy, the boxwoods, and the architecture you paid for.

Local Considerations for Nashville

Light-trespass complaints are real here. In 12 South, East Nashville, and Sylvan Park, lot lines often sit twelve to fifteen feet from a foundation, which means a 35-degree uplight aimed without thought will wash a neighbor's siding by accident. We use shielded optics, hex louvers, and tighter 10–24 degree beam spreads on side-yard fixtures so the spill stays on your bark and your bed wall. On clay-heavy properties near the Brentwood line we also bury transformers above the seasonal water table and seal every wire-nut with gel-fill connectors — the slab-and-crawlspace mix common across older Nashville neighborhoods makes that detail non-negotiable.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Will my uplights spill into a neighbor's window on a tight 12 South lot?

    +
    Not the way we aim them. On 12 South and similar close-set streets we default to 10-degree narrow optics with hex louvers on every side-yard fixture, then walk the line at dusk with the homeowner so any beam touching a property line gets re-aimed or swapped before sign-off. If the line is genuinely fifteen feet from the trunk, we will recommend a downlight from the canopy instead of a trunk uplight.

    Does Nashville's clay soil cause problems for low-voltage wire?

    +
    The clay east of I-65 holds water from December through March, which is exactly why we use gel-filled wire connectors on every splice and bury home-runs in conduit through any low spot. Transformers go on the foundation wall above the seasonal water line, never in the bed itself. Without those steps, copper splices in Nashville clay corrode within five winters.

    Can you uplight a mature oak in Belmont without damaging the root flare?

    +
    Yes — we set fixtures eighteen to twenty-four inches off the trunk on a stake driven outside the critical root zone, never bolted to the bark. For a Belmont specimen oak we typically use three small spots at 120-degree spacing rather than one large uplight, which keeps the canopy lit evenly and the root flare untouched.
